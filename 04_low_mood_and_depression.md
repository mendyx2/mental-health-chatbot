# Low mood and depression

Everyone feels sad sometimes. Depression is different: low mood or loss of interest that lasts most of the day, nearly every day, for two weeks or more, and affects daily life.

## Common signs
Feeling sad, empty, hopeless or numb. Losing interest in things you used to enjoy. Tiredness and low energy. Sleeping too much or too little. Eating much more or much less. Difficulty concentrating or deciding. Feeling worthless or guilty. Pulling away from people. In some people, irritability is the main sign.

## Depression is not weakness
Depression is a health condition. It is not laziness, lack of faith, or a character flaw. It is common, and people recover with support.

## Small steps that help
Keep a simple daily routine: wake up, wash, eat and sleep at regular times.
Behavioural activation: depression makes you want to do less, and doing less makes mood worse. Each day, plan one small activity that gives either pleasure (a call with a friend, music, a favourite meal) or achievement (washing clothes, a ten-minute walk, one study task). Start very small and notice how you feel afterwards.
Get daylight and gentle movement every day.
Stay in contact with at least one trusted person, even briefly.
Break big tasks into tiny steps and celebrate finishing them.
Avoid alcohol and other drugs, which usually make low mood worse.

## When to get help
Please speak to a doctor or mental health professional if low mood lasts two weeks or more, is getting worse, or affects your studies, work or relationships. If you ever have thoughts of ending your life or harming yourself, this is urgent: see the crisis and safety information and contact emergency services at once.
