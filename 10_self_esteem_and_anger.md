# Self-esteem and anger

## Low self-esteem
Low self-esteem means having a persistently negative opinion of yourself. Comparing yourself with others, especially on social media, often makes it worse because people show only their best moments.

Helpful steps: notice your inner critic and answer it the way you would answer a friend; write down three things you did well each day, however small; set small achievable goals; limit comparison; spend time with people who respect you; and practise saying no without guilt.

## Managing anger
Anger is a normal emotion. It becomes a problem when it leads to shouting, violence or damage to relationships.

Early signs: tight jaw or fists, hot face, fast heartbeat.
What to do: pause before reacting; step away from the situation; breathe slowly (in 4, out 6); walk or exercise to release energy; wait until you are calm before speaking; use "I feel... when... because..." statements instead of blame.
Afterwards, think about what the anger was telling you, such as a need for respect, rest or fairness.

If anger leads to hurting others or yourself, or you feel unable to control it, please seek support from a professional. If someone is in immediate danger, call 112.
