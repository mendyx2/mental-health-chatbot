# Panic attacks

A panic attack is a sudden wave of intense fear with strong body sensations such as pounding heart, shortness of breath, chest tightness, trembling, sweating, dizziness, tingling hands, and a feeling that something terrible will happen or that you are losing control. It usually peaks within about 10 minutes and then fades. Although it feels frightening, a panic attack itself is not dangerous.

## What to do during a panic attack
1. Remind yourself: "This is a panic attack. It will pass. I am safe."
2. Sit down if you can and loosen tight clothing.
3. Breathe slowly: in for 4 seconds, out for 6 seconds. Fast breathing makes the sensations stronger.
4. Ground yourself: press your feet into the floor and name 5 things you can see around you.
5. Do not fight the wave. Let it rise and fall while you keep breathing slowly.

## After a panic attack
Rest, drink water, and be kind to yourself. Try not to avoid the place where it happened, because avoidance can make attacks more likely.

## Important safety note
Chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting or symptoms that are new, severe or do not settle should be checked urgently by a medical professional, because they can also have physical causes. If in doubt, go to the nearest hospital or call 112. If panic attacks keep coming back, speak to a doctor.
