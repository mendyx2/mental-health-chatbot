# Sleep problems

Sleep and mental health affect each other. Poor sleep can worsen anxiety and low mood, and worry can make it hard to sleep. Occasional bad nights are normal.

## Healthy sleep habits
Go to bed and wake up at about the same time every day, including weekends.
Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and as cool as comfortable.
Avoid phones and screens for 30 to 60 minutes before bed.
Avoid caffeine (coffee, tea, energy drinks) and heavy meals late in the day.
Use the bed only for sleep, not for studying or scrolling.
Get daylight and some physical activity during the day.

## If you cannot sleep
Do not lie awake watching the clock. If you are awake for about 20 minutes, get up, sit somewhere dim and do something calm (gentle stretching, quiet music, slow breathing) until you feel sleepy, then return to bed.
Write tomorrow's worries and to-do list on paper before bed to get them out of your head.
Try progressive muscle relaxation: tense each muscle group for 5 seconds then relax it, from your feet up to your face.

## When to get help
If sleep problems last for more than a few weeks, affect your daytime functioning, or come with low mood, anxiety or heavy snoring and pauses in breathing, speak to a doctor.
