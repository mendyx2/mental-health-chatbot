# Stress, study pressure and burnout

Stress is the body's reaction to pressure. Short-term stress can help you perform, but long-term stress without rest can lead to exhaustion, anxiety, low mood and illness.

## Signs of too much stress
Headaches, tight shoulders, poor sleep, tiredness, irritability, difficulty concentrating, forgetting things, changes in appetite, feeling overwhelmed, or losing motivation.

## Managing exam and study stress
Plan: list everything you need to do, then pick the three most important tasks for today. Break large topics into small sessions.
Study in blocks: for example 25 to 40 minutes of focused study, then a 5 to 10 minute break away from your phone.
Sleep: all-night studying usually reduces memory and performance. Aim for regular sleep, especially before an exam.
Eat and drink regularly, and move your body daily, even a short walk.
Talk to a friend, classmate or family member rather than carrying the pressure alone.
On exam day: prepare your things the night before, breathe slowly while waiting, and if your mind goes blank, pause, breathe, and start with the question you know best.
Remember that one result does not define your worth or your future.

## Burnout
Burnout is deep exhaustion from long-term stress, often with cynicism and feeling that nothing you do matters. Rest, reducing workload where possible, asking for support, and setting boundaries are important. If it lasts, speak to a health professional.
