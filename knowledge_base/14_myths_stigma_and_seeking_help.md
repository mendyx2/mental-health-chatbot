# Myths, stigma and seeking help

## Common myths
Myth: Mental illness is a sign of weakness. Fact: It is a health condition that can affect anyone, and seeking help shows strength.
Myth: It is caused only by spiritual problems or a curse. Fact: Many factors are involved, such as biology, stress, life events and relationships. Faith and community can be a real source of comfort and can sit alongside medical care.
Myth: People with mental illness are dangerous. Fact: Most are not violent, and they are more likely to be harmed than to harm others.
Myth: Talking about suicide makes it more likely. Fact: Talking openly and kindly can reduce risk and open the door to help.
Myth: Mental health problems do not get better. Fact: Many people recover or manage well with support and treatment.

## Why people delay seeking help
Fear of being judged, shame, cost, not knowing where to go, or believing they should cope alone. These are understandable, but earlier support usually leads to better outcomes.

## How to take the first step
Talk to someone you trust. Visit a doctor or health centre and describe your symptoms and how long they have lasted. You can take a family member or friend with you. Ask questions and say if you do not understand something.

## What to expect
A professional will listen, ask about your symptoms, sleep, appetite, mood and daily life, and discuss options such as talking therapy, lifestyle support or other treatments. Only a qualified health professional can diagnose a condition or advise on medicines.
