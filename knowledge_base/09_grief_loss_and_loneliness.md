# Grief, loss and loneliness

## Grief
Grief is the natural response to loss, such as the death of someone close, the end of a relationship, losing a job or moving away. There is no right way or time limit to grieve. People may feel sadness, anger, guilt, numbness, tiredness, difficulty sleeping or concentrating, or waves of emotion that come and go.

What can help: talk about the person or the loss with someone who listens; keep simple routines; eat and rest; allow tears and other feelings; take part in cultural, family or faith rituals that bring comfort; and be patient with yourself. Anniversaries and special days can be hard, so plan support for those days.

Seek help if grief feels unbearable, stops you functioning for a long time, or you have thoughts of harming yourself.

## Loneliness
Loneliness is the painful feeling of being disconnected, and it can happen even around people. It can affect mood and health.

Small steps: send one message or call to someone each day; join a group, class, faith community or sports activity; volunteer; spend time with others even in quiet ways, such as studying together; and share honestly with one trusted person. Connection grows slowly, so start small.
