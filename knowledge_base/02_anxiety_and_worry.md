# Anxiety and worry

Anxiety is the body's alarm system. A little anxiety is normal, for example before an exam, an interview or a big decision. It can become a problem when worry is frequent, hard to switch off, or gets in the way of sleep, studies, work or relationships.

## Common signs of anxiety
Physical signs: racing or pounding heart, fast breathing, sweating, shaking, tight chest, upset stomach, tense muscles, headaches, feeling dizzy.
Thinking signs: constant "what if" thoughts, expecting the worst, trouble concentrating, mind going blank.
Behaviour signs: avoiding places or tasks, seeking constant reassurance, being irritable, restlessness.

## What can help right now
Slow breathing: breathe in through the nose for 4 seconds, pause for 2, breathe out slowly through the mouth for 6 seconds. Repeat for 3 to 5 minutes. A longer out-breath helps the body calm down.
Grounding with the senses (5-4-3-2-1): name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.
Write the worry down, then ask: is this a problem I can act on today, or a "what if" I cannot control? For problems, write one small next step. For "what ifs", practise letting the thought pass.
Move your body: a short walk or light stretching burns off stress energy.
Reduce caffeine and energy drinks, which can make anxiety feel worse.

## Longer-term habits
Regular sleep and meals, daily movement, limiting constant news and social media, talking to someone you trust, and facing feared situations in small steps instead of avoiding them. Avoidance feels better in the short term but usually makes anxiety stronger over time.

## When to get professional help
If anxiety lasts for several weeks, is stopping you from doing normal things, comes with frequent panic attacks, or you feel hopeless, please speak to a doctor or a mental health professional. Anxiety is common and treatable.
