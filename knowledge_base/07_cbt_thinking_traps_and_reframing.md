# Thinking traps and reframing (basic CBT skills)

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is based on the idea that our thoughts, feelings and actions are linked. When we notice unhelpful thinking patterns and gently challenge them, our feelings can ease.

## Common thinking traps
Catastrophising: expecting the worst possible outcome. ("If I fail this test, my life is over.")
All-or-nothing thinking: seeing things as perfect or a total failure.
Mind reading: assuming you know what others think. ("They think I am stupid.")
Overgeneralising: using words like "always" and "never". ("I always mess up.")
Emotional reasoning: believing something is true because it feels true. ("I feel useless, so I am useless.")
Self-blame: taking responsibility for things outside your control.
Discounting the positive: ignoring good things that happened.

## How to reframe a thought (4 steps)
1. Notice and write the thought. Example: "I failed the quiz, so I am a failure."
2. Name the trap. This one is all-or-nothing thinking and overgeneralising.
3. Look at the evidence. What supports the thought? What does not? What would I say to a friend in this situation?
4. Write a more balanced thought. Example: "I did badly on one quiz. It is disappointing, but I can find out what went wrong and improve."

The aim is not forced positive thinking. It is realistic, kinder and more balanced thinking.

## The thought, feeling, action link
Thought: "Nobody wants to talk to me." Feeling: sad and lonely. Action: stay away from people, which confirms the thought. Changing one part, such as sending one friendly message, can start to change the cycle.
