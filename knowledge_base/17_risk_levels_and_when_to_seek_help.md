# Screening levels: when risk is low, moderate or high

This chatbot uses a simple threshold based on the number of different features of mental health problems that a person has reported during the conversation. The features counted are: low mood, mood swings, excessive laughing or unusually high mood, abnormal behaviour, sleep change, appetite or energy change, withdrawal, anxiety or panic, and poor concentration.

## Low level (one or two features)
The person reports one or two features, usually mild or recent. Suggested response: reassure, explain that many people have such experiences, give simple self-help steps (regular sleep, talking to someone trusted, exercise, relaxation), and advise seeing a counsellor or doctor if the features last two weeks or more or get worse.

## Moderate level (three or four features)
Several features appear together and probably affect daily life. Suggested response: encourage the person to see a doctor, psychologist or counsellor soon, explain which conditions these features are commonly linked to as a preliminary indication only, and ask whether anyone close can support them.

## High level (five or more features, or any severe feature)
The person is at risk when five or more features appear together, or when any one severe feature is present: hearing voices or seeing things others do not, strong beliefs that people are watching or plotting against them, not sleeping for several days, being unable to care for themselves, or any thought of suicide, self-harm, or harming others. Suggested response: express care, advise the person to see a doctor or go to the nearest hospital now, call 112 in an emergency, and not stay alone.

## Always urgent
Any mention of suicide, self-harm, wanting to die, harming someone else, abuse or immediate danger is treated as high risk no matter how many other features are present.

## Reminder
The screening level is a preliminary indication, not a diagnosis. It is meant to help a person decide when to seek professional care.
