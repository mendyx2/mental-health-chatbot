# How to support a friend or family member

## Starting the conversation
Choose a private, calm moment. Say what you have noticed with care: "I have noticed you seem down lately. I am here if you want to talk." Ask open questions such as "How have you been feeling?"

## What helps
Listen without interrupting or judging. Say that their feelings are real and that you are glad they told you. Ask what would help. Offer practical help such as sitting with them, helping them book an appointment, or going with them. Keep checking in over time.

## What to avoid
Do not say "just snap out of it", "others have it worse", or "you just need more faith". Do not promise to keep secrets if their safety is at risk. Do not try to handle everything alone.

## If you are worried about their safety
Ask directly, calmly and kindly: "Are you thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life?" Asking does not put the idea in their head, and it can be a relief. If they say yes, stay with them, remove access to anything they could use to harm themselves where it is safe to do so, and get urgent help: call 112 or take them to the nearest hospital emergency unit. Involve another trusted adult.

Look after yourself too. Supporting someone is tiring, and you also need rest and support.
